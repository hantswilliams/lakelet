# use case · medallion architecture

Bronze, silver, gold. Same layers, one laptop.

Most lakehouses are organised the same way: raw data lands in bronze, gets cleaned into silver, and is shaped into gold tables people actually query. Lakelet doesn't change that pattern. It changes where each layer runs and what it costs. The layers are Iceberg tables in your bucket, the transformations are the dbt project you already have, and the laptop does everything the gauge says it can.

Bronze. Land it raw, keep every row, run it on a schedule.

bronze · usually red

Bronze is the only layer that is naturally cloud-shaped. It reads from outside systems, it grows without bound, and it runs at 6 a.m. whether anyone opened a laptop or not. In a warehouse you'd give it a small always-on cluster. In Lakelet it's a scheduled lakelet run --select tag:bronze --burst auto with a cap: a worker starts in the bucket's region, pulls the new rows, merges them into the bronze tables, and exits.

The gauge calls most bronze loads red for the honest reason: they are bandwidth-bound, and a worker sitting next to your bucket has a hundred times the bandwidth of a laptop on home Wi-Fi. That is exactly the case burst exists for.

Bronze in practice

QuestionAnswer
How does data get in?DuckDB reads Postgres, files and HTTP APIs directly, so a bronze model can be select * from postgres_scan(...). Loader tools (dlt, Airbyte, Fivetran) that write Parquet or Iceberg to the same bucket work too; Lakelet registers what they write.
Materialisationincremental, MERGE INTO on the source key (DuckDB 1.5.3 Iceberg writes). Append-only tables use plain inserts.
Typical verdictRed for anything over a few GB or anything remote; green for small reference tables.
Where it runsEphemeral worker on a schedule (Team), or a laptop that happens to be open (Local).
Partitioningday(loaded_at) so silver only scans what's new. The gauge reads those partitions to price the next layer.
Who reads itOnly silver models. Bronze is not for people.
No connectors framework yet: bronze is bring-your-own-loader in the beta. Built-in incremental connectors are on the roadmap after the team tier.

Silver. Clean it on the laptop, because that is where the engineer is.

silver · mostly green

Silver is where the work happens: casting types, deduplicating, joining reference data, applying the business's definition of a customer. It is also where engineers iterate fifty times a day, and every one of those iterations is a query. On a cloud warehouse, fifty iterations is fifty warehouse resumes. On Lakelet it's fifty local DuckDB runs against the same Iceberg files, and the bill is zero.

Silver models are incremental over bronze partitions, so a daily run touches a day of data and the gauge calls it green. The exceptions are the honest ones: a backfill after a logic change, or a model that has to re-read a year of events. Those come back yellow or red with a price on them, and you choose.

Silver in practice

QuestionAnswer
Materialisationincremental over bronze's day(loaded_at) partitions; unique_key dedup via MERGE INTO.
Typical verdictGreen for daily increments and dev iteration; yellow for wide joins that spill; red for full refreshes.
Where it runsLaptop for development and small increments; the nightly run rides the same schedule as bronze.
Testsdbt tests run after every model, locally, for free. A failed test blocks the commit to the catalog, so a bad silver never reaches gold.
Dev vs prodSame files, same format. A dev target writes to silver_ana.* in the same bucket; promoting is a dbt target switch, not a migration.
ConcurrencyTwo laptops writing the same table commit through the catalog optimistically; the second retries on top of the first.
The gauge's per-model estimate uses the Iceberg manifests of the upstream bronze tables, so it knows a one-day increment from a full refresh before it runs anything.

Gold. Small, fast, rebuilt whenever anyone asks.

gold · green

Gold tables are the ones people query: daily revenue, a customer 360, DAU by country. They are aggregates of silver, which makes them small, which makes them green. On Lakelet a gold table is rebuilt on a laptop in seconds, and the same laptop answers questions against it, in SQL or in English, without a network round-trip.

Gold is also the layer other tools read. Because it's Iceberg in your bucket under a REST catalog, a BI tool, a Spark job, a notebook or an agent reads gold the same way a laptop does. Nothing is exported, nothing is copied, nothing is "synced to the warehouse".

Gold in practice

QuestionAnswer
Materialisationtable, full rebuild. Gold is small enough that incremental logic isn't worth the bugs.
Typical verdictGreen. If a gold model goes red, it's usually silver's job in disguise.
Who reads itEveryone: the Lakelet app, BI tools through lakelet catalog serve or the Team catalog, Spark/Trino, notebooks, agents over MCP with a daily cap.
AccessOn Team, catalog roles grant gold to the company and silver/bronze to the data team; credentials are vended per table prefix.
Saved questionsA question saved in the app becomes a gold model with tests and a commit, so the mart grows from what people actually ask.
FreshnessTeam alerts if gold is older than its schedule promises, or if any test on the path from bronze failed.
Gold is where "your laptop is the warehouse" stops being a slogan: the tables are on the laptop, in the open format, and the questions never leave it.
# the project

It's a dbt project. That's the point.

There is no Lakelet DSL. The layers are folders, the layer rules are a few lines of dbt_project.yml, and the only Lakelet-specific file sets burst defaults per layer. Move the project to Snowflake tomorrow and the models still run.

dbt_project.yml
models:
  acme_analytics:
    bronze:
      +schema: bronze
      +materialized: incremental
      +incremental_strategy: merge
      +partition_by: day(loaded_at)
      +tags: [bronze]
    silver:
      +schema: silver
      +materialized: incremental
      +incremental_strategy: merge
      +tags: [silver]
    gold:
      +schema: gold
      +materialized: table
      +tags: [gold]

# silver/events.sql, the incremental bit
{% if is_incremental() %}
  where loaded_at >

{% endif %}
lakelet.toml
[project]
name      = "acme-analytics"
warehouse = "s3://acme-lake"

[catalog]
mode = "team"   # local|team|external

[burst]
default = "prompt"  # prompt|auto|never
backend = "aws"
region  = "us-east-1"

# per-layer overrides, by dbt tag
[burst.tags.bronze]
default = "auto"
max_cost_per_run_usd = 2.00

[burst.tags.silver]
max_cost_per_run_usd = 5.00

[burst.tags.gold]
default = "never"   # red gold = fix silver

[schedules.nightly]
at     = "06:00"
select = "tag:bronze tag:silver tag:gold"
burst  = "auto"

[agents]
allow = ["query", "estimate",
         "save_question"]
cap_usd_per_day = 2.00
# one weekday

What a Tuesday looks like.

Five-person team, 38 GB across the three layers, one scheduled run and a working day of humans and one agent. Every line is a real gauge decision; the day's cloud bill is at the bottom.

06:00
$0.06

Nightly schedule, bronze → silver → gold

An M worker in us-east-1 merges the day's Stripe and event data into bronze, then silver and gold run on the same worker while it's up. 9 models, 31 tests, 6 minutes, then it exits. Cap was $2.00.

09:10
$0.00

Ana opens her laptop and pulls the catalog

Her local cache refreshes the gold tables (1.1 GB). Ten lakelet sql queries while she checks yesterday's numbers, all green, all local.

10:30
$0.00

Ben iterates on silver.charges

Forty-two runs of one model against one day of bronze over two hours. Each one green, two seconds, tests included. On the old warehouse this was forty-two resumes of a Small warehouse.

11:45
$0.00

Priya in finance asks a question

"Refunds as a share of revenue by month this year." Lakelet writes the SQL against gold.daily_revenue, shows it, runs it locally in 0.3 s, charts it. She saves it; it becomes a gold model in a PR.

14:20
$0.19

Ben's sessionisation change needs a year of events

--full-refresh on silver.events: 31 GB across 365 partitions, 22 minutes of download on his laptop or 90 seconds on an L worker. He sees the $0.30 cap, types y, and keeps working.

15:00
$0.00

A wide join goes yellow

Ana's ad-hoc join of events to customers wants 14 GB on a 16 GB laptop. The gauge says "runs here, slowly, ~3 min, spills to disk; burst ~40 s for $0.08". She waits the three minutes.

16:30
$0.11

A coding agent answers a support ticket

Claude Code, over MCP, calls estimate then query on gold to pull one customer's usage. Green, 1.8 s. Later it tries something on bronze; red, cap $0.30; it bursts once and stops well inside its $2.00 daily cap.

02:00
$0.09

Ops job: compaction and snapshot expiry

Small files from the day's incremental merges are rewritten into larger ones and snapshots older than 30 days are expired, on a worker, so tomorrow's manifests stay small and the gauge stays accurate.

total
$0.45

The day's cloud compute

Five people, one agent, one full refresh, one scheduled run, one ops job. Everything else was laptops. This was a busy day; most have no burst at all, and a month of this team comes to about nine bursts and a few dollars of compute on top of five Team seats.

# what changes

The same medallion, minus the parts that only existed to keep a cluster busy.

If you've built this on Databricks or Snowflake, the layers, the dbt project and the tests are the same. These are the parts that move.

Warehouse-hosted medallionLakelet
Where bronze loads runAn always-on or auto-resuming cluster; billed while awakeA scheduled worker in your bucket's region; billed for minutes, capped, then gone
Where silver is developedThe cluster, one resume per iterationThe engineer's laptop, free, same files as production
Where gold is readThe warehouse, through its own connectorsAnything that speaks Iceberg REST: the app, BI, Spark, notebooks, agents
StorageVendor-managed, often vendor-formatDelta on Databricks, FDN on Snowflake, MotherDuck's own storageApache Iceberg on Parquet in your bucketreadable by every engine, including theirs
CatalogUnity Catalog / Snowflake's, per vendorIceberg REST: Lakelet's, or your Polaris, Lakekeeper, S3 Tables
Dev / prodSeparate schemas on the same cluster; dev still billsDev target writes to silver_ana.* in the same bucket; costs nothing
BackfillsSize up the cluster, hope, size down laterRed verdict with a price; a worker sized to that one run
Cost visibilityMonthly invoice, per-query attribution is extra workEvery run prints estimate, actual and cap; agents and schedules carry their own caps
LeavingExportPoint another engine at the bucket

Where this stops being a fit

Streaming bronze. Kafka-fed, minute-latency bronze isn't on Lakelet's roadmap. If bronze is a stream, keep the streaming layer and let Lakelet own silver and gold over what it lands.

Terabyte-scale silver. When every silver run is red, you are running a warehouse on burst. Lakelet still avoids idle time and still leaves you in Iceberg, but the free laptop tier stops doing most of the work. See where Lakelet isn't the cheapest.

Hundreds of concurrent readers on gold. Publish gold to your bucket and let a serving layer or a BI tool read it directly; that's what the open catalog is for.

Bring your dbt project. Keep your layers.

Private beta on macOS and Linux. The medallion above runs on the free tier; add Team when a second laptop needs the same catalog.
