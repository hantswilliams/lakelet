# pricing

Free where the work happens. Paid where it can't.

The laptop is the warehouse, and the laptop is free. You pay for two things only: a catalog your team shares, and minutes of a bigger machine when a run needs one. Every burst shows its maximum cost before it starts, and cannot exceed it.

How the cap works

Before a burst, the gauge estimates run time on the chosen worker. The cap is that estimate times two, rounded up to a clean number, and you see it before you confirm.

The control plane converts the cap into a wall-clock budget for that worker size. The worker's agent kills DuckDB when the budget is reached and returns a partial-progress error. You can raise the cap deliberately; it cannot be exceeded accidentally.

For scheduled and agent-driven runs, the cap comes from lakelet.toml (per project) or LAKELET_AGENT_CAP_USD (per agent, per day). Past the daily cap, agent calls are refused with the remaining budget.

Nothing runs in the cloud without a number on the screen and a click, or a number in a config file you wrote.

Where Lakelet is not the cheapest

Tiny teams on BigQuery. Under a few hundred GB with modest scan volume, BigQuery's free TiB and no-warehouse billing can land near or below a Team subscription. Lakelet's case there is control and portability, not price: your files stay Iceberg in your bucket, and nothing decides for you.

Above a terabyte or two. When most runs need more than a laptop, most runs burst, and you are paying for cloud compute plus 15%. At that point a reserved warehouse can be cheaper per hour. Lakelet still avoids idle time, but the free tier stops doing most of the work.

Heavy concurrent BI. Fifty people refreshing dashboards all day is a warehouse-shaped workload. Lakelet serves it by publishing models to your bucket and letting your BI tool read Iceberg directly, which works, but it is not what the gauge was built for.

# billing questions

Questions people ask before they put in a card.

Is there a minimum, a commitment, or a per-seat floor on Burst?

No. Burst is billed per second of worker time, per run, with no minimum. You can be on the free Local tier and burst once a year.

Can the bill ever exceed the cap?

No. The cap is enforced by the worker itself, not by an alert after the fact. When the budget is reached the run is killed and you get a partial-progress error and a bill at or under the cap. Raise the cap and rerun if you want it to finish.

What happens if a spot worker is reclaimed?

The run is retried once on on-demand capacity within the same cap. If it still cannot finish inside the cap, it fails and you are billed only for time used.

Can I run workers in my own AWS account?

Yes. Bring-your-own-account bursts run in your account and appear on your AWS bill; Lakelet charges the 15% on the compute it orchestrated. This keeps data, compute and billing inside your boundary.

What does Team store on your side?

Catalog metadata (table schemas, snapshot pointers, commit history), job specs and metrics. Never data files. Delete your Team workspace and your bucket still contains a complete, readable Iceberg lakehouse.

What changes when the beta ends?

Early Team pricing is locked for anyone who joins during the private beta. Local stays free and open source; that is the point of the product, not a promotion.
